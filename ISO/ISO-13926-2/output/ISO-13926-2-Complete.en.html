Document Overview

ISO 13926-2:2017 — Role of the plunger stopper in the pen-injector system

Introduction & Context

ISO 13926-2:2017 is the third edition of the international standard specifying material, performance requirements, and labelling for plunger stoppers used in pen-injectors for medical use. It was prepared by Technical Committee ISO/TC 76 (Transfusion, infusion and injection, and blood processing equipment for medical and pharmaceutical use).

This third edition cancels and replaces the second edition (ISO 13926-2:2011), incorporating Amendment ISO 13926-2:2011/Amd.1:2015. The key change in this edition is that dimensions d1, d2, and d3 in Table 1 were reclassified from normative to informative — recognizing that these diameters must be derived from the selected glass cylinder (ISO 13926-1) and seal (ISO 13926-3).

"Primary packaging components made of elastomeric materials are an integral part of medicinal products and thus, the principles of current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP) apply to the manufacturing of these components." — ISO 13926-2:2017, Introduction

The standard operates within a three-part system:

  • ISO 13926-1 — Glass cylinders (cartridges) for pen-injectors
  • ISO 13926-2 — Plunger stoppers (this standard)
  • ISO 13926-3 — Seals for pen-injectors

System validation is required per ISO 11608-3 (Finished containers), which ties together all three components.

System Role of the Plunger Stopper

What is a Plunger Stopper?

The plunger stopper is the rubber sealing component at the rear end of a pen-injector cartridge (glass cylinder). When the user turns the dose-setting ring and presses the button, the push rod advances the plunger stopper, forcing the drug solution through the front seal and the needle to be injected.

The plunger stopper is the rubber sealing component at the rear of the pen-injector cartridge. The dose mechanism's push rod advances it forward, expelling the drug through the front seal and needle.

Real-life Analogy

Think of the rubber tip on the plunger of a syringe. The plunger stopper of a pen-injector works in exactly the same way, with one difference: it is designed to glide precisely inside the glass cartridge many times (one dose per injection), so consistency of the gliding force is far more critical than in an ordinary syringe.

Think of a syringe's rubber plunger tip — the pen-injector plunger stopper performs identically, but must slide reliably across many repeated doses.

cGMP Applicability

Elastomeric primary packaging components such as plunger stoppers are regarded as part of the drug product, so these components must be manufactured in accordance with cGMP principles (ISO 15378 or the EC GMP Guide). This means a CDMO cannot be exempted from in-depth qualification of the plunger stopper material simply because "the supplier provides a CoA".

Standards Ecosystem Integration

ISO 8871 Series: The Core Standards for Elastomeric Closures

ISO 8871-1 specifies requirements for aqueous autoclavates (extractables), and links directly to the chemical requirements for plunger stoppers (Clause 8.3).

ISO 8871-4 specifies biological requirements, including cytotoxicity and pyrogen tests, and links to the biological requirements for plunger stoppers (Clause 8.4).

This means the E&L framework that plunger stoppers must meet is led by ISO 8871-1 and is not defined directly by ISO 13926-2; ISO 13926-2 incorporates these requirements by reference.

ISO 11608-3: The Key Link for System-Level Validation

ISO 11608-3 (Finished containers) is the standard for system-level testing once the three components (glass cylinder, plunger stopper, seal) are assembled. It covers:

  • Full system testing of initiating force and sustaining force
  • Dose accuracy verification
  • Container closure integrity assessment

Compliance of the plunger stopper alone with ISO 13926-2 is not sufficient; the assembled system must pass ISO 11608-3.

Key Insight for CDMOs

Although USP <381> (Elastomeric Closures for Injections) does not appear in the normative references of ISO 13926-2, for pen-injector drug products sold in the US market the pharmaceutical company usually still needs to perform elastomer testing against USP <381>. This is an additional layer of regulatory compliance beyond the scope of the ISO standard.

Terms & Classification

Clauses 3–4 — No defined terms; three plunger stopper types

Clause 3: Terms and Definitions

No terms and definitions are listed in this document. ISO and IEC maintain terminological databases at:

  • IEC Electropedia: http://www.electropedia.org/
  • ISO Online browsing platform: http://www.iso.org/obp

Clause 4: Classification

Plunger stoppers shall be classified as follows:

  • Type A1: Plunger stoppers with ribs
  • Type A2: Plunger stoppers without ribs
  • Type A3: Plunger stoppers with ribs and dome

Designation Example:

Designation of a plunger stopper Type A1 complying with this document, for a glass cylinder with inner diameter 6.85 mm:

Plunger ISO 13926-2 – 6.85 – A1

The designation uses: word "plunger" + reference to this document + inner diameter of glass cylinder (d2) + type letter.

Practical Significance of the Three Types

Function of the Ribs

Types A1 and A3 have a ribbed design. The ribs are distributed around the outer diameter of the plunger stopper and form several annular contact seals. Their functions are:

  • Sealing: the ribs create localized high-pressure contact against the glass wall, enhancing the liquid-tight/gas-tight seal
  • Gliding balance: the ribs reduce the contact area between the plunger stopper and the glass wall, lowering static friction and making the initiating force more stable
  • Silicone oil distribution: the grooves between the ribs can hold silicone oil, providing sustained lubrication

Type A2 (without ribs) is generally used where specific drug compatibility or process requirements call for it.

Dome Design of Type A3

Type A3 has a dome, a curved surface that protrudes toward the drug solution, which can:

  • Minimize dead volume
  • Improve complete expulsion of the drug solution, which is especially important for high-value biologics
  • Match the shape of the push-rod head to ensure even force transmission

Designation Logic

The d2 in the designation is the inner diameter of the glass cylinder, not the outer diameter of the plunger stopper itself. The design intent is that the dimensions of the plunger stopper must be derived from the specification of the matching glass cylinder, not specified independently. This reinforces "system design" thinking: a plunger stopper cannot be purchased on its own while ignoring the glass cylinder specification.

Engineering Logic of Dimensions

Why Are All Three Diameters "Informative"?

The key revision in this edition (the third edition) is that d1, d2 and d3 were changed from normative to informative. The reasons are:

  • The outer diameter of the plunger stopper must be slightly larger than the inner diameter of the glass cylinder, creating an interference fit that maintains the seal
  • The exact amount of interference depends on the hardness of the rubber formulation, the amount of siliconization, and the actual tolerances of the glass cylinder
  • Therefore the optimal values of d1/d2/d3 can only be derived from the specification of the matching glass cylinder and cannot be preset as normative values

The values in Table 1 are typical examples, offered as a design starting point, not mandatory requirements.

Interference Fit

Table 1 shows that d1 (the maximum diameter of the plunger stopper) is always larger than the corresponding inner diameter of the glass cylinder. For example, for a glass cylinder inner diameter of 6.85 mm, d1 min = 7.1 mm, an interference of about 0.25 mm. After the elastomer is compressed, this interference generates sealing pressure and is the basis of "freedom from leakage".

Practical Issues with Spacers

When plunger stoppers are stacked together in bulk packaging, without spacers the rubber surfaces in contact may stick together (blocking) through the interaction of silicone oil and rubber chemistry. The spacer height is limited to 0.3 mm, a balance between "preventing blocking" and "avoiding an effect on filling accuracy". When receiving plunger stoppers, a CDMO should check for blocking, which may indicate insufficient siliconization or improper storage conditions.

Designation & Material

Clauses 6–7 — Designation convention and elastomeric material requirements

Clause 7: Material

Plunger stoppers shall be made from the elastomeric formulation originally tested and approved by the end-user. The manufacturer shall ensure the conformance of each delivery with the type sample and compliance with previously agreed functional and compendial requirements.

The elastomeric material shall withstand two sterilization cycles when autoclaving in saturated steam at (121 ± 2) °C for 30 min without impairment of its function under normal use conditions.

In case of other sterilization methods (e.g., irradiation), the suitability of the material has to be evaluated.

Key Material Principles:

  • Formulation must be pre-approved by end-user (drug manufacturer)
  • Each batch must conform to the approved type sample
  • Must withstand 2x autoclaving at 121°C/30 min
  • Alternate sterilization (gamma, e-beam) requires specific evaluation

Rubber Compound Selection

Special Considerations for Protein Drugs

For protein biologics (such as GLP-1 analogues, insulin, and monoclonal antibody pen-injectors), the following need particular evaluation when selecting the plunger stopper formulation:

  • Protein adsorption: proteins may adsorb onto the rubber surface, affecting potency
  • Silicone oil particles: excessive siliconization may cause silicone oil particles to enter the drug solution and trigger protein aggregation
  • Antioxidant leaching: some rubber formulations contain antidegradants that may leach out and affect protein stability
  • Zinc leaching: some vulcanization systems contain zinc, and zinc ions may affect protein structure

Importance of End-User Approval

ISO 13926-2 requires the plunger stopper formulation to be approved by the "end-user" (the pharmaceutical company). For a CDMO, if the client has specified a particular supplier and formulation, the CDMO must not substitute on its own. If the supplier needs to be changed, a formal change control must be initiated and client approval obtained again; this is a point of responsibility that must be made explicit in the CDMO contract.

Requirements

Clause 8 — Physical, chemical, and biological requirements including gliding force

Clause 8.2: Physical Requirements

8.2.1 Hardness

The agreed hardness shall not differ from the nominal value by more than ±5 Shore A when tested per ISO 7619-1 on special test specimens.

Alternatively, testing on plunger stoppers per ISO 48 (microhardness) shall not differ by more than ±5 IRHD from the type sample.

8.2.2 Freedom from Leakage

Cartridges shall be free from leakage at the plunger when tested in accordance with Annex A (normative leakage test).

8.2.3 Initiating and Sustaining Forces

These forces are influenced by all components of the container closure system and process parameters (e.g., siliconization). Testing of complete systems is described in ISO 11608-3. Results depend on configuration and pre-treatment (dry/liquid medium, storage time, etc.).

8.2.4 Resistance to Ageing

The maximum time between manufacture date and pharmaceutical use should be agreed between manufacturer and user. Plunger stoppers shall maintain performance characteristics throughout the entire shelf life of the medicinal product, tested as part of stability studies.

NOTE: Ageing depends on storage and handling conditions. Guidance per ISO 2230.

Clauses 8.3 & 8.4: Chemical & Biological Requirements

8.3 Chemical requirements: The requirements in ISO 8871-1 shall apply. (Extractables in aqueous autoclavates)

8.4 Biological requirements: The requirements in ISO 8871-4 shall apply. (Biological requirements and test methods)

Clause 9: Labelling

Packed plunger stoppers which meet the requirements of this document can be marked with the designation given in Clause 6 (e.g., "Plunger ISO 13926-2 – 6.85 – A1").

Key Quality Attributes in Depth

Gliding Force: The Most Critical Functional Parameter

ISO 13926-2 mentions "initiating and sustaining forces" in Clause 8.2.3 but deliberately directs system-level force testing to ISO 11608-3. The reason is that the gliding force of a plunger stopper is not an independent attribute; it is the system output of the whole container closure system (glass cylinder + plunger stopper + siliconization).

Initiating Force: the force required to start the stationary plunger stopper moving (dominated by static friction)

Sustaining Force: the force required to keep the plunger stopper moving (dominated by dynamic friction)

For pen-injectors: an initiating force that is too high makes injection feel difficult for the patient; an unstable sustaining force leads to inaccurate dosing.

Siliconization Balance: A Double-Edged Sword

Siliconization is the application of silicone oil to the outer surface of the plunger stopper to reduce friction. The balance in the amount of silicone oil:

  • Too little silicone oil: gliding force too high, so injection is difficult and dosing is inaccurate
  • Too much silicone oil: silicone oil particles enter the drug solution, causing protein aggregation and increased immunogenicity risk ↑
  • Uneven silicone oil: force fluctuation, so dosing is unstable

The optimal amount of siliconization is usually in the range of a few mg/cm². The specific value must be determined through system validation and written into the specification.

E&L Risk Assessment Framework

Per ISO 8871-1 (chemical requirements), plunger stoppers must be tested in autoclaved aqueous extracts:

  • Extractables: chemicals that can be extracted under simulated or exaggerated extraction conditions (qualitative/quantitative list)
  • Leachables: substances that migrate into the drug solution under actual use conditions (the actual amounts after contact with the drug product)

For protein biologics, E&L assessment needs to pay particular attention to:

  • Antioxidants (BHT, Irganox), which may trigger oxidative degradation
  • Vulcanization accelerators, which are potentially mutagenic
  • Zinc and tin, whose metal ions affect protein stability

The biological requirements of ISO 8871-4 include tests for cytotoxicity, pyrogen (pyrogen/LAL), and hemolysis.

Ageing & Shelf Life

The "expiry date" of the plunger stopper and the "expiry date" of the drug product are two different concepts and must be aligned. The plunger stopper has its own storage validity period after manufacture and before assembly; after assembly, its performance must be maintained through the end of the entire shelf life of the drug product. This requires the CDMO to also verify container closure integrity in stability studies, and not only monitor the drug solution itself.

A

Annex A: Leakage Test (Normative)

The mandatory test method for freedom from leakage at the plunger

A.1 Principle

Water-filled cartridges are prepared using the plunger stoppers to be tested. By means of a suitable device, a force is applied to the plunger stopper during a defined time interval. Any observed leakage is recorded.

The leakage test of plunger stoppers and seals (ISO 13926-3) can be combined.

A.2 Apparatus

  • Cartridge cylinders with silicone-treated inner surface per ISO 13926-1
  • Seals per ISO 13926-3
  • Plungers per this document (ISO 13926-2)
  • Suitable equipment to prepare water-filled cartridges
  • Cartridge holder (e.g., per ISO 11608-3)
  • Device capable of applying the calculated force

A.3 Procedure

A.3.1: Take 10 cartridges and fill them with water (practically air-free) using the plunger stoppers to be tested. Water may be replaced by a coloured solution to improve visibility of leakage.

A.3.2: Place the first cartridge (mounted in cartridge holder) into the pressurizing device. Apply force F (calculated per Formula A.1) for 1 minute. Check for leakage at the plunger stopper.

Formula A.1 — Force Calculation

F = 0.64 × d2²

Where:
  F   = force to be applied (Newton, N)
  d2  = inner diameter of glass cylinder (mm)
        per ISO 13926-1
  0.64 = correction factor (N/mm²)

Example: For d2 = 8.65 mm: F = 0.64 × (8.65)² = 0.64 × 74.82 = 47.9 N

SAFETY PRECAUTIONS: Adequate safety measures should be in place to protect the operator.

A.3.3: Repeat the operation on the remaining 9 cartridges.

A.4 Expression of Results

Report the number of leakages observed at the plunger stoppers. Report if the testing of plunger stoppers and seals (ISO 13926-3) was combined.

Engineering Analysis of Leakage Test

Statistical Considerations of the Sample Size n=10

Testing with 10 cartridges reflects a preliminary functional screening, not a comprehensive statistical quality assurance. In actual CDMO production validation, the sample size for the first batches (PPQ/PV) often needs to be far greater than 10, with statistical methods (such as tolerance intervals) used to confirm the batch conformance rate.

"Self-Sealing" vs "Externally Pressurized" Testing

The test principle of Annex A is to apply a static force to the plunger stopper from the push-rod direction, simulating the instantaneous pressure during injection, and to confirm that the sealing interface does not leak. This differs from other container closure integrity (CCI) test methods (such as vacuum decay and helium leak), which evaluate the integrity of the whole system (including the front seal). The two types of test each have their place and do not replace one another.

Recommendations for CDMO Laboratory Set-up

If a CDMO needs to perform the Annex A test in-house (as part of incoming inspection or process validation), it needs:

  • A precise force application device (force resolution of at least ±1 N)
  • A cartridge holder conforming to ISO 13926-1
  • A timer (1 minute holding time)
  • A colored aqueous solution (to make leakage easier to see)
  • Safety protection measures (risk of glass breakage)

Physical Meaning of the Force Calculation

The coefficient 0.64 N/mm² in Formula A.1 is a representative pressure value (approximately 0.064 MPa or 0.64 bar); multiplying it by the cross-sectional area of the glass cylinder gives the applied force F.

Calculation examples (by size):

A larger cartridge inner diameter requires a larger test force to ensure the same level of sealing performance.

Plunger Stopper Selection Criteria

Key CDMO Decision Nodes

Node 1: Supplier Selection and Qualification

Plunger stoppers are usually supplied by a small number of specialist manufacturers (such as West Pharmaceutical Services, Datwyler, Aptar). During qualification, the CDMO should:

  • Request a declaration of conformity to ISO 13926-2 from the supplier
  • Audit the supplier's ISO 15378 cGMP certification (primary packaging materials)
  • Obtain the complete E&L study data (in accordance with ISO 8871-1 and -4)
  • Confirm that the formulation has been approved by the client (the pharmaceutical company)

Node 2: System Compatibility Verification

Once the plunger stopper is selected, system-level validation (ISO 11608-3) must be performed with the matching glass cartridge (ISO 13926-1) and seal (ISO 13926-3):

  • Confirmation of the gliding force range
  • Container closure integrity (CCI)
  • Dose accuracy
  • Drug-device compatibility studies

Node 3: Incoming Batch Control

Standard checks when the CDMO receives each batch of plunger stoppers:

  • CoA verification (lot number, hardness, appearance)
  • Consistency of the supplier model with the approved formulation
  • Storage conditions met (temperature, humidity; refer to ISO 2230)
  • Packaging integrity (vacuum packaging not damaged)
  • The agreed period from plunger stopper manufacture date to use date has not been exceeded

If a batch-to-batch hardness deviation of more than ±5 Shore A is found, a deviation investigation should be initiated.

Node 4: Special Considerations for GLP-1 Pen-Injectors (Current Hot Topic)

With the surge in demand for GLP-1 receptor agonist (semaglutide, tirzepatide) pen-injectors, a CDMO taking on such projects needs to pay extra attention to:

  • Assessment of the sensitivity of GLP-1 peptides to silicone oil particles
  • Stability of the plunger stopper over a long shelf life (typically 24-36 months)
  • Reseal integrity under repeated puncture in multi-dose pen-injectors
  • Gliding force consistency at high injection volumes (0.5-1.0 mL/dose)

Practice Questions

Five scenario-based questions to reinforce learning

Q1. Force Calculation

A CDMO is validating a new pen-injector cartridge system using a 9.25 mm internal diameter glass cylinder (per ISO 13926-1). According to Annex A (Formula A.1) of ISO 13926-2, what force F (in Newtons) should be applied to the plunger stopper during the leakage test? Show your calculation.

A CDMO is validating a pen-injector system with a glass cartridge of 9.25 mm inner diameter. Per ISO 13926-2 Annex A, what force F should be applied in the leakage test?

Show Answer

F = 0.64 × d2² = 0.64 × (9.25)² = 0.64 × 85.5625 = 54.8 N

This force is applied for 1 minute to each of the 10 test cartridges. Any observed leakage is recorded and reported.

Q2. Type Selection

A pharmaceutical company is developing a high-value monoclonal antibody pen-injector with a 3 mL glass cartridge. They want to minimize drug product wastage (dead volume) while maintaining leak-tight performance. Which plunger stopper type (A1, A2, or A3) would you recommend and why? What additional design consideration must be validated at the system level?

A pharmaceutical company is developing a 3 mL monoclonal antibody pen-injector and wants to minimize dead volume waste. Which plunger stopper type do you recommend? Why? What else needs to be verified at system level?

Show Answer

Recommend Type A3 (with ribs and dome): the dome protrudes toward the drug solution, minimizing residual dead volume at the end of injection and ensuring complete expulsion of the drug solution, which is especially important for high-value monoclonal antibodies. The ribs also provide good sealing and stable gliding force.

System-level verification (per ISO 11608-3): the dome shape must match the geometry of the push-rod tip to ensure even force transmission; dose accuracy must also be verified, because the dome design must not cause dose deviation in the last dose.

Q3. E&L Risk Assessment

During a drug-device compatibility study, your team detects a trace amount of Irganox 1076 (a phenolic antioxidant used as a rubber additive) in a GLP-1 peptide drug product stored in a pen-injector cartridge for 6 months. ISO 13926-2 Clause 8.3 mandates compliance with ISO 8871-1. What are your next steps? What specific risk does Irganox 1076 pose to a peptide drug?

In a drug-device compatibility study, a trace amount of Irganox 1076 (a phenolic antioxidant) was detected in a GLP-1 peptide drug product. The ISO 8871-1 requirements apply. What is the next step? What specific risk does Irganox 1076 pose to peptides?

Show Answer

Next steps:

  1. Confirm per ISO 8871-1 whether this substance is on the extractables list and whether the amount exceeds the acceptance criteria
  2. Assess whether its leachable level under the conditions of use of the drug product is toxicologically significant (TTC analysis)
  3. Initiate a deviation investigation: confirm formulation consistency between supplier batches
  4. Assess whether the plunger stopper formulation or supplier needs to be changed
  5. Notify the client (the NDA holder) and assess the impact on the approved specification

Risk of Irganox 1076 to peptides: phenolic antioxidants are strong reducing agents and may cause oxidative modification of the peptide chain, altering the drug's primary structure or affecting disulfide bond integrity, ultimately affecting potency and stability. In addition, if the toxicological threshold is exceeded, there is also a patient safety risk.

Q4. Siliconization Optimization

A CDMO's manufacturing team reports that the initiating force for a new insulin pen-injector is consistently too high (patients report difficulty injecting). The plunger stopper meets all dimensional specs per ISO 13926-2 Table 1. What process parameters should be investigated? How does ISO 13926-2 guide you on where to look for the root cause?

The CDMO manufacturing team reports that the initiating force of an insulin pen-injector is too high (patients complain of difficult injection), while the plunger stopper dimensions meet the specification. Which process parameters should be investigated? How does ISO 13926-2 guide the root cause analysis?

Show Answer

Guidance from ISO 13926-2 (Clause 8.2.3): the initiating and sustaining forces are determined jointly by all components of the container closure system and by the process parameters (especially siliconization), and require system-level analysis per ISO 11608-3.

Investigation priorities:

  1. Amount of siliconization: confirm the amount of silicone oil on the plunger stopper and/or the inner wall of the glass cartridge, and whether it meets the agreed specification
  2. Uniformity of silicone oil distribution: sample and test the uniformity of silicone oil coverage (e.g. by scanning electron microscopy or staining methods)
  3. Glass cartridge surface roughness: batch-to-batch differences in the roughness of the glass inner wall may affect friction
  4. Storage conditions: dry storage vs storage after liquid filling; the gliding force differs significantly between "dry" and "wet"
  5. Plunger stopper batch hardness: confirm whether it is within the ±5 Shore A range

Q5. Standards Integration

A regulatory reviewer asks why the ISO 13926-2:2017 compliance certificate alone is not sufficient evidence that a pen-injector plunger stopper is suitable for a new biologic drug product. Prepare a concise technical explanation (3-4 sentences) referencing the specific related standards that together constitute the full qualification package.

A regulatory reviewer asks: why is an ISO 13926-2 compliance certificate alone not sufficient to demonstrate that the plunger stopper is suitable for a new biologic? Prepare a concise technical explanation citing the relevant standards.

Show Answer

ISO 13926-2 specifies only the material, physical dimensions and minimum performance requirements at the component level of the plunger stopper; it does not cover system-level functional verification (which requires ISO 11608-3) or compatibility with a specific drug. A complete qualification package needs to include:

  • ISO 8871-1: chemical extractables testing, confirming that elastomer leachables do not affect drug product quality
  • ISO 8871-4: biological safety testing (cytotoxicity, pyrogen, etc.)
  • ISO 11608-3: system-level functional verification using the actual combination of cartridge, seal and drug (including gliding force, dose accuracy, CCI)
  • Drug-device compatibility study: assessment of the adsorption and stability impact for the specific biologic (long-term stability studies under the ICH Q1 framework)

If any layer is missing, the suitability of the plunger stopper cannot be adequately supported from a regulatory standpoint.
