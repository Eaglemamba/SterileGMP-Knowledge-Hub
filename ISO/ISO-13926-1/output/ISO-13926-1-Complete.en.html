This fourth edition cancels and replaces the third edition (ISO 13926-1:2004), which has been technically revised. The main change compared to the previous edition is:

  • Changing dimensions d1, d2 and d3 in Table 1 from normative to informative.

Introduction

The potency, purity, stability and safety of a drug during its manufacture and storage can be strongly affected by the nature and performance of the primary packaging.

This document deals with glass cylinders used with pen-injectors in accordance with ISO 11608-1.

The Role of the Pen-Injector and Its Glass Cylinder

ISO/TC 76 - What Does This Technical Committee Cover?

ISO/TC 76 is responsible for standardization of transfusion, infusion and injection equipment, and blood processing equipment. The ISO 13926 series (glass cylinders), the ISO 11608 series (needle-based injection systems) and ISO 23908 (pen needles) all fall within its remit. TC 76 is directly relevant to drug packaging, so it is a standards body that pharma regulatory staff and CDMOs need to know.

Analogy: The Glass Cylinder Is the Drug's "Home"

The structure of a pen-injector can be pictured as a precision mechanical pencil: the glass cylinder is the lead-holder that contains the drug; the plunger stopper (ISO 13926-2) is the rubber head that pushes the liquid out; the seal (ISO 13926-3) is the cap that closes the front end. The three must fit together precisely, and ISO 13926-1 governs this "home", the glass cylinder body.

Key Change in the Fourth Edition: d1/d2/d3 Changed from Normative to Informative

In the third edition (2004), the outer diameter d1, the intermediate diameter d2 and another diameter d3 were mandatory dimensions (normative). The fourth edition (2018) changed these three outer-diameter dimensions to informative only, which gives manufacturers more flexibility. This reflects the diversity of the glass cylinder market: as long as d4-d6 and h1/h2 meet the specification and the design is verified per ISO 11608-3, it is acceptable.

How the Primary Packaging Affects Drug Quality

The Introduction states clearly that the nature and performance of the primary packaging directly affect the potency, purity, stability and safety of the drug. This matters especially for biologics (protein drugs): if the chemical resistance of the glass cylinder is inadequate, metal ions can leach out and cause protein aggregation or degradation.

The ISO 13926 Standards Ecosystem

The ISO 13926 Series - A Trilogy

  • Part 1 (this standard): the glass cylinder, i.e. the drug container body
  • Part 2 (ISO 13926-2): the plunger stopper, i.e. the rubber stopper that drives the drug forward
  • Part 3 (ISO 13926-3): the seal, i.e. the front-end closure that reseals after needle penetration

The three components together form one complete primary container system, which must be validated as a whole (ISO 11608-3).

ISO 720 vs ISO 4802 - Two Test Logics for Hydrolytic Resistance

ISO 720 (grain glass test): the glass is crushed into grains and then tested, reflecting the chemical resistance of the bulk glass material. The best class is HGA 1.

ISO 4802 (surface test): the inner wall of the container is tested directly, reflecting the surface condition of the finished article after processing (because the high temperatures during forming can alter the surface composition). The best class is HC 1.

ISO 13926-1 requires both: the material level (ISO 720 HGA 1) and the surface level (ISO 4802 HC 1), a double safeguard.

Significance of System Verification per ISO 11608-3

Meeting dimensional specifications for a single component is not enough. ISO 11608-3 requires the glass cylinder, plunger stopper and seal to be verified as a system, confirming leakage, glide force, drug compatibility and so on. This is an integrated testing obligation that a CDMO must consider when selecting components.

ISO 11608-1 in the Bibliography

ISO 11608-1 (general requirements for needle-based injection systems) is listed in the Bibliography (informative references) rather than in the normative references. This means it provides a background framework (what a pen-injector is), but this standard does not directly require compliance with all clauses of ISO 11608-1; its definitions are merely referred to in the Introduction.

Bore Geometry Requirements

The dimension d6 (bore diameter) shall be maintained for a depth of h1.

The opening of the glass cylinder should have a constant diameter over the entire distance h1 (cylindrical shape). A slightly conical shape is acceptable if:

  • The truncated cone has height h1;
  • The larger diameter is located at the flange or as agreed upon;
  • The larger diameter does not exceed the smaller one by more than 0.3 mm.

Practical Interpretation of the Dimensions

Function of the Six Key Dimensions

  • d1 (outer diameter): used to match the pen-injector housing; changed to informative, allowing design differences between device manufacturers
  • d4 (flange outer diameter) = 7.15±0.2 mm: the only fixed, mandatory outer diameter! The positioning/retention structure of the pen-injector depends on this dimension
  • d5 (barrel inner diameter) = 5.5±0.35 mm: the mating dimension for the plunger stopper (ISO 13926-2), which determines dose accuracy
  • d6 (orifice diameter) = 3.15±0.2 mm: where the seal (ISO 13926-3) is inserted, affecting penetration force and sealing performance
  • h1 (orifice depth) = 5.0±0.20 mm: the seal insertion depth, ensuring reliable sealing
  • h2 (flange height) = 2.9±0.1 mm: the height of the flange structure, affecting assembly stability

Worth noting: d4/d5/d6/h1/h2 are identical across all four sizes

Look closely at Table 1: whether d1 is 8.65 or 11.60 mm, d4, d5, d6, h1 and h2 are the same for all four sizes! This means the core mating dimensions of the pen-injector (plunger stopper interface, seal interface, flange) are standardized, and only the outer diameters (d1-d3, informative) differ. This allows component manufacturers to make universal plunger stoppers and seals.

Acceptance Criterion for a Tapered Orifice: ≤0.3 mm

In glass forming (especially the tubing process for tubular glass), it is hard to guarantee a perfectly cylindrical orifice, so a slight taper is allowed. However, the upper limit on the difference in diameter due to taper is 0.3 mm, and the larger-diameter end must be on the flange side. This ensures that the seal does not leak or show abnormal penetration force because of excessive taper when inserted.

l1 and l2: To Be Agreed with the Customer

The barrel length (l1) and overall length (l2) have no fixed specification and are agreed between the glass cylinder manufacturer and the customer (usually a pharma company or CDMO). This is because different drug doses (such as insulin 100 U, or a GLP-1 once-weekly dose) require different volumes, and the volume determines the barrel length. Typical commercial sizes range from 1.5 mL to 3.17 mL.

Clause 5: Requirements - Material and Performance Requirements

Material specification (Type I glass), sealing surface, hydrolytic resistance (HC 1), annealing quality

5.1 Material

Colourless (cl) or amber (br) glass of the hydrolytic resistance grain glass in accordance with ISO 720 – HGA 1 shall be used.

It shall correspond to the glass type 1 of the relevant pharmacopoeia.

A change in the chemical composition of the glass material shall be notified to the user at least nine months in advance.

The glass material used for glass cylinders shall not contain seeds or bubbles to an extent which will interfere with the visual examination of the contents.

5.2 Performance

5.2.1 Sealing Surface

Glass cylinders shall have a sealing surface which is flat and free from ripples or undulations.

5.2.2 Hydrolytic Resistance

When tested in accordance with ISO 4802-1 or ISO 4802-2, the hydrolytic resistance of the internal surface of the glass cylinder shall comply with the requirements for class HC 1 container class.

Before conducting the test, the bottom end of the cylinder shall be sealed with a suitable closure element (e.g. silicone rubber).

5.2.3 Annealing Quality

After the glass cylinder is annealed, the maximum residual stress shall not produce an optical retardation exceeding 40 nm per millimetre of glass thickness, when the glass cylinder is viewed in a strain viewer.

The optical retardation test method shall be agreed upon between glass manufacturer and customer.

Material Performance Summary Table

Why Type I Borosilicate Glass

Type I Glass - The Gold Standard for Pharmaceutical Packaging

The approximate chemical composition of Type I borosilicate glass is: SiO₂ 70-80%, B₂O₃ 7-13%, Al₂O₃ 2-7%, Na₂O/K₂O <5%. The addition of boron greatly improves the chemical resistance of the glass, keeping it stable with acidic, neutral and mildly alkaline drug solutions.

  • Chemical resistance: does not react with water for injection, proteins or pH buffers
  • Low extractables: leaching of metal ions such as Na⁺ and Ca²⁺ is very low and does not affect drug stability
  • Thermal stability: withstands steam sterilization in an autoclave (121°C)
  • Transparency: allows visual inspection of the appearance of the drug solution

HGA 1 vs HC 1 - Two Complementary Indicators

HGA 1 (ISO 720): ground glass grains (0.3-0.5 mm) are autoclaved at 121°C for 60 minutes, and the extracted alkali is titrated with hydrochloric acid. HGA 1 means the alkali extracted per gram of glass is ≤0.10 mL of 0.01 mol/L HCl.

HC 1 (ISO 4802): the inner surface of the container is tested directly, with the extracted amount expressed as μg Na₂O/mL. HC 1 is the highest resistance class (≤0.10 μg/mL).

Why are both needed? Because the high temperature of glass forming (≈1000°C) can cause segregation of the surface composition: HGA 1 ensures raw material quality and HC 1 ensures the surface performance of the finished article. Neither can be omitted.

Siliconization - Not Explicitly Specified in the Standard, but Critical in Practice

The body of ISO 13926-1 does not specify siliconization requirements, but almost all glass cylinders in the industry carry a dimethicone (dimethyl silicone oil) silicone layer on the inner wall. The functions of siliconization are:

  • Reduces plunger glide force: the injection force of a pen-injector directly affects patient experience, and siliconization can bring the glide force down from >20 N to <10 N
  • Prevents plunger stopper sticking: during long-term storage the plunger stopper may bond to the glass, and siliconization prevents this
  • Risk: excess silicone oil may produce visible particles (silicone droplets) and make the injection solution cloudy, so the amount of silicone must be controlled (usually 0.5-1.5 mg/mL of surface)

The amount and uniformity of siliconization fall within the manufacturer's process control and must be confirmed in the ISO 11608-3 system verification.

Annealing Quality - Why It Matters

If the glass cylinder cools unevenly after forming, residual stress is created within the glass. Excessive residual stress can lead to:
(1) spontaneous breakage of the glass during storage or use;
(2) breakage under the increased pressure during injection;
(3) contamination of the drug solution by glass particles.

Annealing is the slow, uniform cooling of glass in an annealing lehr to relieve residual stress. The optical path difference limit of ≤40 nm/mm is a safety threshold based on historical practice.

9-Month Change Notification - Safeguarding Supply Chain Stability

A change in glass composition may affect extractables and leachables (the E&L study would need to be repeated) and compatibility (drug stability studies would need to be re-validated). A 9-month advance notice gives the drug manufacturer enough time to start change control and to submit SUPAC (Scale-Up and Post-Approval Changes) documentation to FDA/EMA and other authorities. This is an important clause in pharma purchasing contracts.

Clause 6 — Designation

Glass cylinders for pen-injectors for medical use shall be designated by:

  • Reference to this document (ISO 13926-1)
  • Colour of the glass: cl (colourless) or br (amber/braun)
  • Nominal diameter d1 expressed in millimetres

Clause 7 — Package Marking

The package shall be marked with the following information:

  1. The designation of the cylinders (per Clause 6)
  2. The identifying number (if cylinders are printed), e.g. lot, batch, etc.
  3. The number of cylinders it contains
  4. The name or symbol of the manufacturer

Designation Decoding Exercise

Note: the designation uses d1 (an informative dimension) as the identifying name, but compliance requires the normative dimensions such as d4-d6, so the naming system and the compliance system use different dimensional bases.

When to Use Amber Glass

Amber glass ("br", from the German Braun) blocks light at wavelengths of 290-450 nm and protects light-sensitive drugs. In pen-injectors, insulin usually uses clear glass (cl) because patients need to observe the condition of the solution (cloudy/clear); some GLP-1 drugs or biologics that are light-sensitive may use amber glass. However, with amber glass the solution cannot be inspected visually, and other methods (such as cosmic-ray inspection) are needed to verify the contents.

Package Marking - A Minimum Requirement, Not the Full Set of GMP Requirements

The 4 package marking requirements in ISO 13926-1 are a minimum standard. In practice, pharma or CDMO customers usually require more information:

  • Manufacturing date and shelf life
  • Storage conditions (temperature, humidity)
  • Sterilization status (if sterilized)
  • Reference to the raw material certificate (CoA, Certificate of Analysis)
  • Barcode/QR code (for automated warehousing)

These additional requirements are usually written into the purchasing specification or the supplier quality agreement (SQA).

GLP-1 drug surge, pen-injector demand, glass cylinder selection considerations for CDMOs

Pen-Injector Glass Cylinder: Market Drivers

The global pen-injector market has experienced explosive growth driven by:

  • Insulin therapy — long-established; multi-dose cartridges (3 mL) are standard
  • GLP-1 receptor agonists — semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy), liraglutide (Victoza, Saxenda), dulaglutide (Trulicity)
  • Growth hormone — somatropin for pediatric and adult GHD
  • Other biologics — parathyroid hormone, calcitonin, etc.

CDMO Perspective: Considerations in Choosing Glass Cylinder Specifications

Impact of the GLP-1 Boom on the CDMO Glass Cylinder Business

The GLP-1 drug boom of the 2020s (Ozempic, Wegovy, etc.) has tightened global glass cylinder supply. The major suppliers (Schott, Gerresheimer, Nipro, SGD) cannot expand capacity quickly, which has led to:

  • Lead times extending from the standard 12-16 weeks to 24-36 weeks
  • Drug companies and CDMOs needing to lock in supply volumes 18-24 months in advance
  • Some GLP-1 companies turning to the prefilled syringe format as an alternative

For a CDMO, mastering the detailed ISO 13926-1 specifications is what makes it possible to judge compliance quickly when evaluating multiple suppliers.

Sterilization Strategy for Glass Cylinders - Not Specified by ISO 13926-1, but Must Be Considered in Practice

ISO 13926-1 itself does not specify sterilization requirements for glass cylinders (sterilization belongs to the fill-finish process). However, a CDMO needs to consider:

  • Depyrogenation: dry-heat tunnel oven, usually 250°C ×30 minutes (FH≥170), to eliminate endotoxin
  • Washer: rinsing with water for injection to ensure particles meet Ph. Eur. or USP requirements
  • Siliconization integrity: whether the silicone layer is still intact after depyrogenation, which needs to be validated
  • EtO vs radiation sterilization: EtO is generally not used for glass cylinders and dry heat is the mainstream; assembled sub-components may use gamma irradiation

System Integration Logic: The Three Parts Are Inseparable

Imagine the glass cylinder system of an injection pen:
The glass cylinder (ISO 13926-1) is the body of a water bottle;
the plunger stopper (ISO 13926-2) is the rubber pusher inside the bottle that moves forward to push the water out;
the seal (ISO 13926-3) is the silicone plug in the bottle spout, which releases the drug once pierced by the needle.
The three parts must match in dimensions, be compatible in materials and be coordinated in dynamic performance, and that is the meaning of ISO 11608-3 system verification.

Practice Questions

5 questions covering dimensions, material requirements, system integration, and CDMO applications

Practice Questions (5 in total)

Question 1 (Dimensions)
According to ISO 13926-1 Table 1, which of the following statements is correct?
A. The d4 (flange outer diameter) differs among the four sizes
B. d1, d2 and d3 are mandatory normative dimensions
C. The d5 (barrel inner diameter) is 5.5±0.35 mm for all four sizes
D. l1 and l2 have fixed specifications and need no agreement
Answer: C. d4 is 7.15±0.2 mm in all sizes (the same value); d1/d2/d3 became informative from the fourth edition onward; l1/l2 are agreed between the manufacturer and the customer.

Question 2 (Material Requirements)
ISO 13926-1 requires the glass to meet both ISO 720 HGA 1 and ISO 4802 HC 1. Why must both be met, rather than just one?
Answer: ISO 720 HGA 1 tests the hydrolytic resistance of the raw glass material itself (ground grains); ISO 4802 HC 1 tests the hydrolytic resistance of the inner wall surface of the finished container. The two may not agree, because the high-temperature forming process can alter the glass surface composition (surface delamination risk). Dual testing ensures that both the material and the finished surface reach Type I quality.

Question 3 (Orifice Geometry)
ISO 13926-1 allows the opening of the glass cylinder to be slightly tapered. What conditions limit the taper?
Answer: three conditions must be met at the same time: (1) the height of the truncated cone = h1; (2) the larger-diameter end is on the flange side (or as agreed); (3) the larger diameter must not exceed the smaller diameter by more than 0.3 mm. This ensures that the seal still seals reliably when inserted, and that excessive taper does not cause leakage.

Question 4 (Annealing Quality)
In a strain viewer test, a batch of glass cylinders with a glass wall thickness of 1.2 mm shows an optical path difference of 52 nm. Does this batch meet ISO 13926-1 requirements? Explain why.
Answer: It does not. ISO 13926-1 requires an optical path difference of ≤40 nm/mm. Converting: 52 nm ÷ 1.2 mm = 43.3 nm/mm, which exceeds the 40 nm/mm limit. This indicates insufficient annealing and excessive residual stress, with a risk of breakage, so the batch should be rejected and returned or re-annealed.

Question 5 (CDMO Practice)
A CDMO is evaluating pen-injector filling services for a customer's GLP-1 drug (semaglutide). The customer wants to use a glass cylinder with d1=11.6 mm. List at least 4 key items that must be confirmed when selecting the glass cylinder supplier and specification, and explain the reason for each.
Sample answer: (1) HC 1 hydrolytic resistance CoA: protein drugs are extremely sensitive to ion leaching, which affects stability; (2) siliconization specification (amount, distribution uniformity): affects injection force and particle risk; (3) annealing quality data (optical path difference pass rate): ensures no breakage during storage and use; (4) whether the 9-month change notification clause is written into the purchasing contract: avoids unannounced material changes undermining the validity of drug stability data; (5) system verification compatibility (ISO 11608-3): the mating specifications of the plunger stopper and seal suppliers must be confirmed to match this glass cylinder.
