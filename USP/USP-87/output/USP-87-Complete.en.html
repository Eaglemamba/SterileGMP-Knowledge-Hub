Learning Objectives

  • Understand the purpose and scope of the USP <87> in vitro biological reactivity tests, and the principles for choosing among the three test methods
  • Master the specific requirements for L-929 cell culture preparation and for setting up positive and negative controls
  • Know how the procedures differ for the Agar Diffusion Test, the Direct Contact Test and the Elution Test
  • Read the reactivity grading table correctly (Grade 0–4) and know that the acceptance criterion is Grade ≤ 2
  • Understand the logic behind extraction solvent selection and the equipment requirements (autoclave, oven, incubator)
  • Link USP <87> to USP <88>: materials that fail the in vitro tests must go on to in vivo testing
  • Understand the business significance of material compliance assessment for container closure systems in CDMO pharmaceutical manufacturing
Section 1: Introduction & Scope

Original Text

The following tests are designed to determine the biological reactivity of mammalian cell cultures following contact with the elastomeric plastics and other polymeric materials with direct or indirect patient contact or of specific extracts prepared from the materials under test. It is essential that the tests be performed on the specified surface area. When the surface area of the specimen cannot be determined, use 0.1 g of elastomer or 0.2 g of plastic or other material for every mL of extraction fluid. Exercise care in the preparation of the materials to prevent contamination with microorganisms and other foreign matter.

Three tests are described (i.e., the Agar Diffusion Test, the Direct Contact Test, and the Elution Test). The decision as to which type of test or the number of tests to be performed to assess the potential biological response of a specific sample or extract depends upon the material, the final product, and its intended use. Other factors that may also affect the suitability of a sample for a specific use are the polymeric composition; processing and cleaning procedures; contacting media; inks; adhesives; absorption, adsorption, and permeability of preservatives; and conditions of storage. Evaluation of such factors should be made by appropriate additional specific tests before determining that a product made from a specific material is suitable for its intended use. Materials that fail the in vitro tests are candidates for the in vivo tests described in Biological Reactivity Tests, In Vivo 〈88〉.

Tutorial Commentary

Key Concepts

Biological Reactivity Test: A series of tests that evaluate whether elastomers, plastics and other polymeric materials cause cytotoxicity when in contact with human cells. It is the core test in the biocompatibility assessment of a container closure system (CCS).

Logic for choosing among the three test methods:

  • Agar Diffusion Test: suitable for elastomeric closures of any shape
  • Direct Contact Test: suitable for materials of medium density; extraction and testing take place at the same time
  • Elution Test: suitable for high-density materials and for dose-response evaluation

Surface area conversion rule: where the surface area cannot be determined → elastomers 0.1 g/mL; plastics 0.2 g/mL of extractant

Analogy

Imagine you want to test whether a new rubber stopper is "safe". You cannot put it into a human body to find out, so you first bring it into contact with "human cells in a culture dish" and watch how the cells react. If the cells survive and their morphology stays normal, the material is biocompatible; if many cells die, further animal testing (USP <88>) is needed. This is the tiered "in vitro → in vivo" evaluation logic.

Key Notes

Relationship between <87> and <88>: USP <87> is the first screening gate (in vitro); a material that fails must undergo the USP <88> animal tests (in vivo). The compliance strategy is to pass <87> first and so avoid the <88> animal tests, which is more efficient both from the regulatory and the ethical point of view.

Materials covered: elastomers that contact the patient directly or indirectly (such as rubber stoppers and diaphragms), plastics (such as syringes and IV bags) and other polymeric materials.

  2. If an Elution Test result shows Grade 3 (Moderate), what should be done next?
  3. Why can USP <87> only screen for biological toxicity and not fully replace USP <88>?
Section 2: Test Controls

Original Text

Positive control: Polyurethane film containing zinc diethyldithiocarbamate (ZDEC) or zinc dibutyldithiocarbamate (ZDBC)

Cell culture preparation: Prepare multiple cultures of L-929 (ATCC cell line CCL 1, NCTC clone 929; alternative cell lines obtained from a standard repository may be used with suitable validation) mammalian fibroblast cells in serum-supplemented minimum essential medium having a seeding density of about 105 cells per mL. Incubate the cultures at 37 ± 1° in a humidified incubator for NLT 24 h in a 5 ± 1% carbon dioxide atmosphere until a monolayer, with greater than 80% confluence, is obtained. Examine the prepared cultures under a microscope to ensure uniform, near-confluent monolayers.

[Note—The reproducibility of the in vitro biological reactivity tests depends upon obtaining uniform cell culture density.]

Extraction solvents: Sodium Chloride Injection [see monograph—use Sodium Chloride Injection containing 0.9% of sodium chloride (NaCl)]. Alternatively, serum-free mammalian cell culture media or serum-supplemented mammalian cell culture media may be used. Serum supplementation is used when extraction is done at 37° for 24 h.

Tutorial Commentary

Key Concepts

L-929 Cell Line: A mouse subcutaneous connective tissue fibroblast cell line, and the standard test cell specified jointly by USP, ISO 10993-5 and other standards. ATCC CCL-1; it has stable proliferation characteristics and high sensitivity to cytotoxic substances.

Positive Control (ZDEC/ZDBC): A polyurethane film containing zinc diethyldithiocarbamate that reliably causes a cell reaction of ≥ Grade 3 (Moderate). Purpose: to verify that the test system is functioning properly.

Key cell culture parameters:

  • Seeding density: about 105 cells/mL
  • Culture conditions: 37 ± 1°C, 5 ± 1% CO₂, humidified atmosphere
  • Incubation time: NLT 24 h (at least 24 hours)
  • Confluence requirement: > 80% confluence

Analogy

Setting up the test controls is like the "answer check" system of an exam. The positive control (ZDEC) is like a "deliberately wrong question": you know it must show a toxic reaction (≥ Grade 3), otherwise something is wrong with the whole test system. The negative control (HDPE or blank extractant) is like the "standard non-toxic answer" and must show Grade 0 (no reaction). If neither control meets expectations, the data for the whole test batch are invalid and the test must be repeated.

Key Notes

System suitability criteria:

  • Negative control → Grade 0 (no reaction) ✓
  • Positive control → at least Grade 3 (Moderate) ✓
  • Both must be met at the same time; otherwise the test must be repeated

Extraction solvent selection: 0.9% NaCl (isotonic physiological saline) is the first choice; if the extraction is at 37°C/24 h, serum-supplemented culture medium must be used to prevent protein denaturation.

Practice Questions

  1. If the positive control shows only Grade 2, is the result of your test batch valid? How should it be handled?
  2. The L-929 cell monolayer is only 65% confluent. Can the test proceed?
  3. Why must serum-supplemented culture medium be used for extraction at 37°C?
Section 3: Apparatus

Original Text

Autoclave: Employ an autoclave capable of maintaining a temperature of 121 ± 2°, equipped with a thermometer, a pressure gauge, a vent cock, a rack adequate to accommodate the test containers above the water level, and a water cooling system that will allow for cooling of the test containers to about 20°, but not below 20°, immediately following the heating cycle.

Oven: Use an oven, preferably a mechanical convection model, that will maintain operating temperatures in the range of 50°–70° within ±2°.

Incubator: Use an incubator capable of maintaining a temperature of 37 ± 1° and a humidified atmosphere of 5 ± 1% carbon dioxide in air.

Extraction containers: Use only containers, such as ampuls or screw-cap culture test tubes, or their equivalent, of Type I glass. If used, culture test tubes, or their equivalent, are closed with a screw cap having a suitable elastomeric liner. The exposed surface of the elastomeric liner is completely protected with an inert solid disk 50–75 µm in thickness. A suitable disk can be fabricated from polytef.

Preparation of apparatus: Cleanse all glassware thoroughly with chromic acid cleansing mixture and, if necessary, with hot nitric acid followed by prolonged rinsing with Sterile Water for Injection. Sterilize and dry by a suitable process for containers and devices used for extraction, transfer, or administration of test material. If ethylene oxide is used as the sterilizing agent, allow NLT 48 h for complete degassing.

Tutorial Commentary

Key Concepts

Type I Glass: Borosilicate glass, which has the highest chemical inertness and does not release into the extractant any components that could interfere with the test. It is the only material specified by USP for extraction containers in biological tests.

Three types of extraction equipment correspond to three temperature conditions:

  • Autoclave: 121°C / 60 min (most severe)
  • Oven: 70°C / 24 h or 50°C / 72 h
  • Incubator: 37°C / 24 h (mildest, used to simulate physiological conditions)

The higher the extraction temperature, the more thorough the extraction, but the more likely the sample is to undergo physical changes (melting/fusing).

Specifications

Extraction conditions at a glance:
Autoclave: 121 ± 2°C, 60 min
Oven (high temperature): 70 ± 2°C, 24 h
Oven (medium temperature): 50 ± 2°C, 72 h
Incubator: 37 ± 1°C, 24 h (serum-supplemented culture medium)

Cooling after extraction: cool to room temperature, but not below 20°C
Extract storage: 20°C–30°C, use within 24 h

Key Notes

Strict requirements for glass cleaning: Glassware must be treated with chromic acid cleaning solution and, where necessary, hot nitric acid, and finally rinsed with sterile water for injection. Residues of ordinary detergents can interfere with the test results.

Degassing for NLT 48 h after ethylene oxide sterilization: EO residues are themselves cytotoxic, and incomplete degassing leads to false-positive results. This is a common compliance risk point in glassware management at a CDMO.

Polytef protective disks: The elastomeric liner of a screw-capped tube must be completely covered with a 50–75 µm polytef (polytetrafluoroethylene) disk to prevent elastomer components from interfering with the extraction results.

Practical Application

On a CDMO aseptic filling line, rubber stoppers (such as lyophilization stoppers) must be qualified under USP <87>/<88> before each batch is used. When choosing extraction conditions, the QC laboratory should base them on the final sterilization conditions of the container: if the product is sterilized by 121°C steam autoclaving, the extraction should also use the 121°C condition, to simulate the worst-case actual contact.

Section 4: Agar Diffusion Test

Original Text

This test is designed for elastomeric closures in a variety of shapes. The agar layer acts as a cushion to protect the cells from mechanical damage while allowing the diffusion of leachable chemicals from the polymeric specimens. Extracts of materials that are to be tested are applied to a piece of filter paper.

Sample preparation: Use extracts prepared as directed, or use portions of the test specimens having flat surfaces NLT 100 mm2 in surface area.

Positive control preparation: Proceed as directed for Sample preparation.

Negative control preparation: Proceed as directed for Sample preparation.

Procedure: Using 7 mL of cell suspension prepared as directed in Cell culture preparation, prepare the monolayers in plates having a 60-mm diameter. Following incubation, aspirate the culture medium from the monolayers, and replace it with serum-supplemented culture medium containing NMT 2% of agar. [Note—The quality of the agar must be adequate to support cell growth. The agar layer must be thin enough to permit diffusion of leached chemicals.] Place the flat surfaces of Sample preparation, Positive control preparation, and Negative control preparation or their extracts in an appropriate extracting medium, in duplicate cultures in contact with the solidified agar surface. Use no more than three specimens per prepared plate. Incubate all cultures for NLT 24 h at 37 ± 1°, preferably in a humidified incubator containing 5 ± 1% of carbon dioxide. Examine each culture around each sample, negative control, and positive control under a microscope, using a suitable stain, if desired.

Interpretation of results: The biological reactivity (cellular degeneration and malformation) is described and rated on a scale of 0–4 (see Table 1). Measure the responses of the cell cultures to the Sample preparation, the Positive control preparation, and the Negative control preparation. The cell culture test system is suitable if the observed responses to the Negative control preparation is grade 0 (no reactivity) and to the Positive control preparation is at least grade 3 (moderate). The sample meets the requirements of the test if the response to the Sample preparation is not greater than grade 2 (mildly reactive). Repeat the procedure if the suitability of the system is not confirmed.

Table 1. Reactivity Grades for Agar Diffusion Test and Direct Contact Test

Tutorial Commentary

Key Concepts

The dual function of the agar layer:

  • Physical cushion: prevents the sample from pressing directly on the cells and causing mechanical damage (not chemical toxicity)
  • Chemical diffusion: soluble toxic substances (leachables) can still diffuse through the agar to the cells

This allows the test to distinguish two different injury mechanisms: "chemical toxicity" and "mechanical pressure".

Test specifications:

  • Culture dish: 60 mm diameter, 7 mL of cell suspension
  • Agar concentration: NMT 2% (not more than 2%)
  • Maximum of 3 samples per dish
  • Replicates: duplicate cultures
  • Incubation: NLT 24 h, 37 ± 1°C, 5 ± 1% CO₂

Analogy

The Agar Diffusion Test is like laying a "protective jelly" over the culture dish. The rubber stopper sits on top of the jelly; if it contains toxic substances (leachables), these slowly seep through the jelly and reach the cell layer below. If a "zone of death" appears in the cell area under the jelly, the toxic substances have diffused through. The larger the zone, the higher the Grade and the stronger the toxicity.

Grading Criteria

Grade 0 (None):    no detectable reaction zone → pass
Grade 1 (Slight):  a few malformed cells directly under the sample → pass
Grade 2 (Mild):    reaction zone extends < 0.45 cm → pass (maximum allowed)
Grade 3 (Moderate):reaction zone extends 0.45–1.0 cm → fail
Grade 4 (Severe):  reaction zone extends > 1.0 cm → fail

Acceptance criterion: Sample ≤ Grade 2 (Mildly Reactive)
System suitability: Negative Control = Grade 0
            Positive Control ≥ Grade 3

Key Notes

The key to agar concentration: The agar must support cell growth (acceptable quality) but must not be too thick (otherwise toxic substances cannot diffuse to the cells). NMT 2% is the upper limit; in practice 0.5–1.5% is usually used.

Applicable situations: This test is particularly suitable for elastomeric closures of any shape (rubber stoppers, diaphragms, plungers, etc.), because the agar layer prevents mechanical damage caused by irregular shapes from interfering with the result.

Practical Application

When a CDMO pharmaceutical plant sources a new batch of rubber stoppers from a supplier, QC usually uses the Agar Diffusion Test first as a quick screening tool. If the result is ≥ Grade 3, the lot is rejected immediately and the purchasing department is notified; if it is Grade 1–2, whether to run additional tests depends on the compliance requirements. Acceptable test results should be kept in the batch records to support the container closure system section of the DMF (Drug Master File).

Section 5: Direct Contact Test

Original Text

This test is designed for materials in a variety of shapes. The procedure allows for simultaneous extraction and testing of leachable chemicals from the specimen with a serum-supplemented medium. The procedure is not appropriate for very low- or high-density materials that could cause mechanical damage to the cells.

Sample preparation: Use portions of the test specimen having flat surfaces NLT 100 mm2 in surface area.

Positive control preparation: Proceed as directed for Sample preparation.

Negative control preparation: Proceed as directed for Sample preparation.

Procedure: Using 2 mL of cell suspension prepared as directed in Cell culture preparation, prepare the monolayers in plates having a 35-mm diameter. Following incubation, aspirate the culture medium from the cultures, and replace it with 0.8 mL of fresh culture medium. Place a single Sample preparation, a Positive control preparation, and a Negative control preparation in each of the duplicate cultures. Incubate all cultures for NLT 24 h at 37 ± 1° in a humidified incubator containing 5 ± 1% of carbon dioxide. Examine each culture around each Sample preparation, a Positive control preparation, and a Negative control preparation, under a microscope, using a suitable stain, if desired.

Interpretation of results: Proceed as directed for Interpretation of results in Agar Diffusion Test. The sample meets the requirements of the test if the response to the Sample preparation is not greater than grade 2 (mildly reactive). Repeat the procedure if the suitability of the system is not confirmed.

Tutorial Commentary

Key Concepts

Characteristics of the Direct Contact Test:

  • Simultaneous extraction and testing: the sample is placed directly on the cell layer, so extraction and toxicity evaluation take place at the same time
  • No agar protective layer: the cells are exposed directly to the sample, giving higher sensitivity
  • Limits on applicable materials: not suitable for materials of very low density (they float and do not press on the cells) or very high density (they crush the cells)

Differences in test specifications (vs. Agar Diffusion):

  • Culture dish: 35 mm (vs. 60 mm)
  • Cell suspension: 2 mL (vs. 7 mL)
  • Culture medium volume: 0.8 mL fresh culture medium
  • Samples: 1 per dish (vs. ≤ 3)

Analogy

The Direct Contact Test places the material sample right "in the cells' face". With no jelly cushion, the sample and the cells are in zero-distance contact. The advantages are a more sensitive and faster reaction; the drawback is that a sample that is too heavy may crush the cells, but that is "physical" damage rather than "chemical" toxicity and must be distinguished from it. For this reason, the Direct Contact Test is not suitable for high-density (very heavy) or low-density (floating) materials.

Key Notes

Interpretation criteria are the same as for the Agar Diffusion Test: Table 1 is used in the same way, and the acceptance criterion is again Sample ≤ Grade 2. The difference lies in the test setup, not in the interpretation criteria.

When to choose it: Suitable for materials of irregular shape that have a flat contact surface (≥ 100 mm²) and medium density, such as some syringe components and flexible plastic sheeting.

Limits of applicability: It cannot be used for: (1) materials of too low density (such as foams and porous materials); (2) materials of too high density (such as metal components); use the Elution Test instead.

Practice Questions

  1. Why does the Direct Contact Test use a 35 mm culture dish rather than a 60 mm one? Consider the effect of cell density.
  2. Which test method should be chosen for a PETG plastic sheet of relatively high density, and why?
  3. How does the flat surface area requirement of the Direct Contact Test (≥ 100 mm²) affect sample preparation for elastomeric closures?
Section 6: Elution Test

Original Text

This test is designed for the evaluation of extracts of polymeric materials. The procedure allows for extraction of the specimens at physiological or nonphysiological temperatures for varying time intervals. It is appropriate for high-density materials and for dose-response evaluations.

Sample preparation: Prepare as directed in Preparation of extracts, using either Sodium Chloride Injection [0.9% sodium chloride (NaCl)] or serum-free mammalian cell culture media as Extraction solvents. If the size of the sample cannot be readily measured, a mass of NLT 0.1 g of elastomeric material or 0.2 g of plastic or polymeric material per mL of extraction medium may be used. Alternatively, use serum-supplemented mammalian cell culture media as the extracting medium to simulate more closely physiological conditions. Prepare the extracts by heating for 24 h in an incubator containing 5 ± 1% of carbon dioxide. Maintain the extraction temperature at 37 ± 1°, because higher temperatures may cause denaturation of serum proteins.

Procedure: Using 2 mL of cell suspension prepared as directed in Cell culture preparation, prepare the monolayers in plates having a 35-mm diameter. Following incubation, aspirate the culture medium from the monolayers, and replace it with extracts of the Sample preparation, Positive control preparation, or Negative control preparation. The serum-supplemented and serum-free cell culture media extracts are tested in duplicate without dilution (100%). The Sodium Chloride Injection extract is diluted with serum-supplemented cell culture medium and tested in duplicate at 25% extract concentration. Incubate all cultures for 48 h at 37 ± 1° in a humidified incubator preferably containing 5 ± 1% of carbon dioxide. Examine each culture at 48 h, under a microscope, using a suitable stain, if desired.

Interpretation of results: Proceed as directed for Interpretation of results in Agar Diffusion Test but use Table 2. The sample meets the requirements of the test if the response to the Sample preparation is not greater than grade 2 (mildly reactive). Repeat the procedure if the suitability of the system is not confirmed. For dose-response evaluations, repeat the procedure, using quantitative dilutions of the sample extract.

Table 2. Reactivity Grades for Elution Test

Tutorial Commentary

Key Concepts

The core difference of the Elution Test:

  • The sample material does not contact the cells directly; only the extract contacts the cells
  • Best suited to: high-density materials (for which the Direct Contact Test cannot be used) and dose-response studies
  • Incubation time: 48 h (longer than the 24 h of the other two tests)

Extract dilution logic (important!):

  • Extraction with serum-supplemented culture medium → 100% undiluted
  • Extraction with serum-free culture medium → 100% undiluted
  • Extraction with 0.9% NaCl → diluted to 25% (diluted with serum-supplemented culture medium)

The NaCl extract must be diluted because plain saline contains none of the nutrients cells need to grow, so using it at 100% concentration is detrimental to the cells.

Analogy

The Elution Test is like a "tea-brewing test for toxicity". You first soak the material in a specific solvent (extraction), then add this cup of "tea" to the cell culture medium: the cells drink the extract and do not bite the material directly. This lets you evaluate which substances can be "brewed out" and are toxic. The reason the NaCl extract is diluted is like a too-concentrated salt environment, which is itself harmful to cells; it must be diluted to a concentration the cells can tolerate so that the effect of toxic substances can be tested fairly.

Elution Test Grading

Grade 0 (None):    intracytoplasmic granules intact, no cell lysis → pass
Grade 1 (Slight):  ≤20% of cells rounded, loosely attached, without intracytoplasmic granules → pass
Grade 2 (Mild):    20–50% of cells rounded, without intracytoplasmic granules → pass (upper limit)
Grade 3 (Moderate):50–70% of the cell layer rounded or lysed → fail
Grade 4 (Severe):  cell layer almost completely destroyed → fail

Acceptance: Sample ≤ Grade 2
Note: Table 2 evaluates the overall state of the cells (unlike the diffusion zone of Table 1)

Key Notes

The fundamental difference between Table 1 and Table 2:

  • Table 1 (Agar Diffusion/Direct Contact): evaluates the size of the "zone of toxic reaction by spatial diffusion"
  • Table 2 (Elution Test): evaluates "overall cell viability" and changes in cell morphology

Dose-Response Study: To confirm the relationship between the concentration of a toxic substance and the reaction, the test can be repeated with a quantitative dilution series (such as 100%, 50%, 25%, 12.5%...) to build a dose-response curve. This is of great value in toxicological assessment.

48 h incubation time: 24 h longer than the other tests, giving subacute toxicity effects enough time to appear.

Practical Application

In CDMO packaging material validation (Container Closure System Validation), IV bags (high-density PP/PE) are usually evaluated under USP <87> by the Elution Test. The choice of extraction solvent takes into account the final dosage form of the drug product: for aqueous preparations, NaCl/culture medium is chosen; for oily preparations (such as fat emulsions), extraction with vegetable oil is needed only at the USP <88> stage.

Section 7: Comparison of the Three Test Methods

Summary Overview

Tutorial Commentary

Decision tree for choosing a test method

  • Elastomeric closures (rubber stoppers, diaphragms) → choose the Agar Diffusion Test first
  • Medium-density plastic sheeting and components → Direct Contact Test
  • High-density materials / need for dose-response data → Elution Test
  • Regulation requires a specific method → follow the regulation

Note: Several tests can be run at the same time, but the regulatory documentation must state the reason for the choice (scientific justification).

Key Notes

Core common points of the three tests:

  • The same L-929 cells are used
  • The same system suitability check is required (negative/positive controls)
  • The same acceptance criterion: Sample ≤ Grade 2
  • The same conclusion: failure → proceed to USP <88>

  2. Direct Contact Test vs. Elution Test: which is more sensitive to volatile toxic substances, and why?
  3. Why is the acceptance criterion for all three tests set uniformly at Grade ≤ 2 rather than Grade ≤ 1?

Tutorial Commentary

Key Concepts

USP High-Density Polyethylene RS (high-density polyethylene reference standard):

This is the officially certified USP negative control material. HDPE (high-density polyethylene) is a plastic widely recognized as having good biocompatibility and contains no known cytotoxic substances. In the test it serves as the negative control that "should show a Grade 0 reaction".

Related ASTM standards:

  • ASTM F 895-84: Standard Test Method for Agar Diffusion Cell Culture Screening for Cytotoxicity
  • ASTM F 813-83: Standard Practice for Direct Contact Cell Culture Evaluation of Materials for Medical Devices

Key Notes

Source of the ZDEC/ZDBC positive controls: Supplied by the Hatano Research Institute, the food and drug safety center in Kanagawa Prefecture, Japan. In actual testing, positive control material that is within its expiry date and from an approved source must be used, and the COA (Certificate of Analysis) must be kept.

Procurement control of USP reference standards: All USP RS (Reference Standards) must be purchased from USP and managed according to their storage conditions to ensure traceability. This is a key item in QA control of a GMP laboratory.

Practical Application

When a CDMO QC laboratory performs USP <87> tests, it must establish the following control procedures: (1) receipt, inventory and usage records for USP HDPE RS; (2) expiry tracking for the ZDEC/ZDBC positive controls; (3) passage number management of the L-929 cell line (usually limited to a certain passage number to ensure test reproducibility). These are all key audit items during FDA/EMEA inspections.

Chapter Summary

  • Purpose: USP <87> determines the in vitro biological reactivity of elastomers, plastics and other polymeric materials using mammalian cell culture systems
  • Three tests: Agar Diffusion Test (first choice for elastomers), Direct Contact Test (medium-density materials), Elution Test (high-density materials/dose-response)
  • Test cells: L-929 mouse fibroblast cell line (ATCC CCL-1), >80% confluence
  • System suitability: negative control = Grade 0; positive control ≥ Grade 3; otherwise the test is invalid
  • Acceptance criterion: uniform for all three tests → Sample ≤ Grade 2 (Mildly Reactive)
  • Grading scales: Table 1 (0–4, spatial diffusion evaluation) is used for Agar Diffusion and Direct Contact; Table 2 (0–4, cell viability evaluation) is used for the Elution Test
  • Upstream/downstream relationship: failing <87> → USP <88> animal testing is required; <87> is the first material safety screening gate
  • Extraction conditions: NaCl injection (first choice), serum-supplemented/serum-free culture medium; temperature: 37°C (physiological) → 50°C → 70°C → 121°C (increasing severity)
  • CDMO business significance: qualification of new supplier materials, batch release testing, and biocompatibility data supporting the container closure system section of a DMF for drug product applications
